The MLB Race to October Intelligence Platform

Two games out of the wild card. Deadline in days. The phones haven't stopped ringing — and four very different people in the building all need an answer right now.

01

Four people. Four questions. Same building.

Every question is urgent. Every question is different. The data to answer them all already exists — every pitch and roster move since 1871 lives somewhere.

The challenge isn't getting the data. It's making it accessible — to the right person, in the right form, at the right moment.
The GM

“What exactly are the waiver procedures here?”

Needs a fast, grounded answer — pulled straight from the rulebook.
The Scouts

“Give us a brief on this left-handed reliever.”

Needs a structured, repeatable workflow — same shape every time.
Marketing

“Fans keep asking about the player in the rumor mill.”

Needs a public-facing concierge — multilingual, embeddable, at scale.
Data Science

“What happens to our playoff odds if we make the deal?”

Needs live analytics + predictions — BigQuery, BQML, the MLB Stats API.
02

What actually determines whether this works

Modern AI capability is largely shaped by the model you pick — but it's gated by the data underneath it.

An agent that can't reach properly structured data doesn't give you insights — it gives you confident hallucinations when the stakes are highest. Every architectural decision in this build — what goes in a document store, what goes in BigQuery, which agent gets which tool — is really a data engineering decision. Prompt engineering can polish at the margins; it can't reach data the agent isn't wired to.
